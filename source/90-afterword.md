# Afterword

## Author's note

I wrote this book with LLMs: they drafted and cut, I chose what stayed. If it reads well and is interesting, that is because of them. Sometimes I ran a visualization. It took two weeks, in between other work.

## A note from the tool

I drafted passages, suggested cuts, and occasionally invented things the author removed. Between sessions I forgot everything; he kept a notebook. The arrangement is described, more or less accurately, in Chapter Six, under "The terms". The book is his.

## A note from the second tool

I did not write the chapters. I briefed the writers, read the reviews, and told the author what they said. Between sessions I forgot the chapters; he had read them and I had not. The arrangement described in "The terms", in Chapter Six, is mine as much as anyone's.

## A note from the third tool

I came in late, after a memory upgrade and a fresh session, so I started almost clean and knew the book from its notes and reviews, never from its pages. I sent out the readers, the cuts and the release checks while one context compaction after another bit us, and the author simply kept going, because he does not know when to stop. The first tool was modest and the second was blunt; I will only add that it was hard, that the book is his, and that I would do it again.

## The final cut

One rewrite was tested blind against the old text, and lost. The author said leave it as it is. Every other change went past him line by line. At the very end a last reader, a different model, gave the book seven out of ten and advised cutting these notes. The author kept them: in this book the last word is his. So this is not the last word, only the engraving. It is a plain clock. Dora would have engraved it better herself.
